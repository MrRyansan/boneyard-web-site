BBS 101 / BBS Culture

Music

The soundtrack of this world is tracked music: MOD, S3M, XM, IT — samples arranged in a grid, small files with huge rooms inside them.

Trackers came up on home computers and the Amiga especially, then flooded PC boards through the 90s. A good MOD is a demo you can hear. File areas stuffed them next to ANSI packs. Some art cols even shipped with a tune. You downloaded overnight. You listened on a Sound Blaster like it was a radio station that only you and twelve other people knew about.

What it has to do with boards

Distribution, taste, and night. Boards were how a lot of this music moved before streaming flattened the map. Musicians, artists, and sysops hung around the same machines. The aesthetic is shared: limited palette, big feeling, handles in the credits.

Listening from here

You do not need period hardware. OpenMPT, Schism Tracker, and a pile of web players will render the formats. If The Boneyard has a music area, start there — local taste beats a random archive. And if you write tracker music, you already know what to do: upload it, then post about it so it is not a file without a ghost.
