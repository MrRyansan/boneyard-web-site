BBS 101 / BBS Culture

Art

BBS art is mostly text that pretends it is not text. Block characters, a 16-color palette, and an 8×16 font (Code Page 437) were enough to make logos, login screens, and entire illustrated ads.

ANSI and ASCII

ANSI uses escape codes for color and cursor motion. It can animate, in a limited, charming way. ASCII is the same instinct without the crayons — shading made of #, :, and stubbornness. RIP graphics and PETSCII are cousins from other neighborhoods. The important part is constraint. Constraint is why it still looks like a time and a place.

Packs and groups

Art groups released monthly packs the way bands released EPs. ACiD, iCE, and a long tail of others competed on taste, speed, and who got to draw which board’s screen. A “colly” is a collection; a “sauced” file carries a metadata footer so the artist’s handle does not fall off in transit.

How to look at it now

Use a client that speaks CP437, not a web browser’s idea of Unicode. SyncTERM and NetRunner are built for this. If the login screen looks like leftover soup, your font is wrong. If it looks like a neon graveyard, you are in the right place.
