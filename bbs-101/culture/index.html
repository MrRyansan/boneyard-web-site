BBS Culture

A board is software. The culture is what people did with 80 columns, 16 colors, and a need to be seen by a dozen other night owls.

Handles instead of legal names. Screens that take longer to draw than to read. Groups that released art packs like albums. A sense that the machine in the spare room was a place, not a service. That is the weather system around BBSes, and it leaked into adjacent worlds — trackers, demos, the underground scene — without ever being exactly the same as any of them.

You do not have to join a group or draw a colly to belong on a board. You do have to understand that someone, somewhere, stayed up until 4 a.m. making the login screen pretty for you.
