How a BBS Works

Under the menus, a board is a host program, a handful of nodes, and a pile of databases. Mystic is the host here. You are a caller on a node.

A node is a seat. Old boards had as many nodes as they had phone lines — often one. Telnet boards can have more. If every node is busy, you wait, or you get a busy signal in spirit. Once you are in, the software paints menus (often ANSI), reads your keystrokes, and lets you into the areas you have access to.

Access is usually tiered. New users are on a short leash until the sysop validates them. That is not snobbery so much as janitorial work: it keeps the dumpsters from arriving before the regulars.

The four rooms

Most boards are the same house with different wallpaper:

  • [1]Message boards — public posts and private mail
  • [2]Files — downloads, uploads, descriptions
  • [3]Doors — games and other programs bolted on the side
  • [4]Chat — live talk when more than one soul is online

Menus are the UI

There is no mouse, or there should not need to be one. Letters and numbers move you around. H or ? is often help. Q or X gets you out. Read the prompt. The prompt is the contract.

When you log off, your last-read pointers stay behind, so next time the board can say “here is what you missed.” That small courtesy is half the addiction.
