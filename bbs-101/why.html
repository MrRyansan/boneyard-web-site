BBS 101

Why go to one?

Because a small room is a different drug from a stadium. A BBS is sized for recognition. People notice if you show up. They notice if you only take.

Slow mail, actual mail

Messages on a board are closer to letters than comments. You write in a thread that might sit overnight. You read the whole thing. Nobody is optimizing your outrage. That slowness is the feature. It makes people slightly more themselves, or at least more deliberate.

A place with a landlord

The sysop is a person with a name, a taste, and a delete key. That can be cramped. It can also be a relief. Culture is not a terms-of-service PDF. It is “we do it like this here.” The Boneyard likes Japan, night, and dark topics handled like adults. If that is your weather, you will feel the walls fit.

Games, files, and craft

Door games are still funny. ANSI still looks like nothing else. File bases still feel like rummaging through a friend’s closet instead of a warehouse. And there is craft in the old sense: people drawing screens, writing loaders, keeping software alive because they want to, not because a company has a roadmap.

Nostalgia is allowed. It is not required.

Some callers remember 2400 baud in their bones. Some have never heard a handshake in their life. Both are valid. You do not need a childhood in the 80s to enjoy a board. You need curiosity and a handle.

If that sounds right, dial The Boneyard. If you want the tour first, keep reading how a BBS works.
