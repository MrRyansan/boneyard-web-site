BBS 101 / How a BBS Works

How a BBS Works

These days you can connect to a BBS using a “terminal client” to connect to them over the Telnet protocol or SSH (secure approach). Modern terminal clients support these methods and most BBSs accept both.

The four things

All BBSs generally have four things that they offer, the main differences being how the board is customized by the SysOp to give it their own personal signature as well as what types of message areas and file areas they offer.

Message Boards

Also called “Message Bases” these are the equivalent of what you would find on Forum sites. They are different areas with different topics that you can post messages in, read messages from others, and reply to them. I think these are the heart of most BBS systems and is where you go to connect with others as well as express yourself.

There are two main types of message boards: Local, and Networked. Local message boards are only specific to the BBS you are on. Networked ones actually sync the messages across all BBSs that partake in that network. On The Boneyard you’ll see networked message bases prefixed with [Net], and local ones with [Local].

There is also private mail that you can send to the SysOp or other users as well which basically acts like Email.

File Areas

Similar to message boards, there are different “File Bases” or File Areas where you can upload and download all sorts of different things. From books to software, you name it.

Doors

A Door is basically an external program that can be run as if it’s a part of the BBS itself. It’s like a “doorway” to an external system or program that sends you there to play a game, or interact with a utility or some other feature, and then returns you back to the BBS when you are done. It is all rather seamless to you, but is actually separate from the BBS itself. For example “Door Games” are games you can play where you click on a game you want to play, it sends you over to the game, and then when you are done, returns you back to the BBS.

Chat

There are three types of chat on a BBS that you can do. You can chat directly with the SysOp of the board, either by paging them (it causes their computer to beep repeatedly for a period of time to alert them that you want to chat), or a SysOp can just suddenly “drop into chat” during your session to chat with you. You can chat with other users who are also logged into a BBS (each user logs into a “node”) and is known as “inter-node chat”. There is also the ability to chat with users who are connected on other BBSs all over the world and is called MRC(multi-relay chat) and is more akin to joining chat groups of today. You can chat with others, join “rooms” of various topics, etc.
